## Concept A — Malibu Magazine

**Route:** `/concept-malibu`

**Summary**  
The most editorial of the three. Warm ivory background, large italic Playfair Display headlines, and full-bleed photography that drives the whole experience. Alternating product rows give it a magazine-editorial feel. The accessories grid and dark-background newsletter are polished and refined. This concept would feel at home next to Aritzia, Sézane, or Arc-en-Ciel.

**Visual direction**  
Photography-first. The layout exists to frame the photos, not the other way around. Almost no decorative UI — just type, space, and image.

**Color palette**  
- Background: warm ivory `#F8F6F2`  
- Text: warm near-black `#1C1714`  
- Accent: sand gold `#C4A882`  

**Typography**  
- Headlines: Playfair Display italic (400 weight)  
- Body: Inter  

**Layout structure**  
Full-bleed hero → editorial about intro (2-col) → alternating product rows → full-width image quote break → accessories grid → dark newsletter → footer.

**Strengths**  
- Premium, aspirational feel that ages well  
- The product photography does most of the heavy lifting  
- Strong for brand storytelling — founder angle fits naturally  
- Closest to the Arc-en-Ciel reference the founder shared  
- Shopify section-mapping is straightforward (each row = one section)  

**Weaknesses**  
- May feel slightly mature/adult for a teenage founder's audience  
- Less social-native — doesn't lend itself to TikTok-style content  
- Depends heavily on having beautiful editorial photography at launch  

**Who it attracts**  
Shoppers who already buy from Arc-en-Ciel, Sézane, Aritzia. They care about quality details, appreciate good taste, and will pay a bit more. Age range 17–28. They follow @arcenatelier, @sezane, quiet luxury accounts.

**Recommended next step**  
If this direction is chosen, invest in one strong editorial shoot (golden-hour, warm tones, 2–3 looks). The layout will carry everything from there.

## Concept B — LA Sporty Angel

**Route:** `/concept-la-sporty`

**Summary**  
The most energetic and social-native of the three. Bold Nunito Black headlines, a split hero, a scrolling marquee ticker, and a scrollable lookbook strip. This concept has momentum — it feels like it was built to be shared. The blush-and-rose palette is feminine without being soft. This concept is the most natural fit for TikTok and Instagram Reels.

**Visual direction**  
Social-native. Every section has energy. The marquee, the lookbook scroll, the pill buttons — it's designed for a generation that swipes fast and reacts immediately.

**Color palette**  
- Background: clean white `#FEFEFE`  
- Text: strong black `#1A1A1A`  
- Blush accent: `#E8B4B8`  
- Rose: `#D4848C`  
- Pill/surface: `#FDE8EA`  

**Typography**  
- Headlines: Nunito Black (900 weight, tight letter-spacing)  
- Body: DM Sans  

**Layout structure**  
Split hero (photo left, headline right) → marquee ticker → asymmetric product grid → scrolling lookbook strip → energy banner → bags section → blush newsletter → footer.

**Strengths**  
- Matches the founder's age and natural energy  
- Strongest social media fit — easy to clip sections for TikTok  
- The scrolling lookbook strip is a standout UI moment  
- Pill buttons and rounded cards are very on-trend for 2025  
- Most likely to attract impulse buyers  

**Weaknesses**  
- Trendy aesthetic means shorter shelf life — may need a redesign in 2–3 years  
- Higher execution risk: needs consistent, high-energy photography  
- Can feel cheap if the photography quality drops  

**Who it attracts**  
Gen Z shoppers who follow Gymshark, Glossier, and activewear drops. They shop via Instagram stories and TikTok links. Age range 14–22. They use #OOTD, they screenshot aesthetics, and they tag brands in their posts.

**Recommended next step**  
If this direction is chosen, film a short 15-second brand video for the hero on launch. Even a simple phone video with good lighting would outperform a still photo here.

## Concept C — Soft Luxury

**Route:** `/concept-soft-luxury`

**Summary**  
The quietest of the three. Pure white background, Cormorant Garamond Light headlines, one product per full-width row. No decorative elements — just typography, negative space, and beautiful photography. This concept communicates brand confidence. It says: "we don't need to sell hard."

**Visual direction**  
Quiet luxury. Maximum white space. Every product gets its own moment. The layout trusts that the product and photography are enough.

**Color palette**  
- Background: pure white `#FFFFFF`  
- Warm surface: `#F8F5F1`  
- Text: warm near-black `#111111`  
- Muted: `#9A9088`  
- Accent: sand `#C8B89A`  
- Rule: `#E4DDD6`  

**Typography**  
- Headlines: Cormorant Garamond (300 weight, wide letter-spacing)  
- Body: Jost (300 weight)  

**Layout structure**  
Full-bleed hero (centered text, bottom) → 2-col opening statement → individual product rows (alternating image/text) → full-width lifestyle image → 4-col accessories grid → warm-background newsletter → minimal footer.

**Strengths**  
- Timeless — this design will still look current in 5 years  
- Strongest brand equity builder  
- Each product gets proper visual weight (good for launch when inventory is small)  
- Very clean Shopify section structure — easiest to map 1:1  
- The footer already includes Shipping / Returns / Privacy links  

**Weaknesses**  
- The quietest of the three — may feel sparse or "too adult" for teenage buyers  
- Requires the most photography discipline — no bright colors, no noise  
- Lower impulse-buy energy compared to Concept B  

**Who it attracts**  
Older end of the target audience (18–28) and international shoppers (Europe, Romania). They shop slowly and deliberately. They follow @toteme, @cos, @arket. They'll pay full price if the brand feels considered.

**Recommended next step**  
If this direction is chosen, start with 3–4 hero products only (don't launch with 12 items spread thin across the rows). Depth > breadth.

## Recommended Direction

All three concepts are technically sound and could go to Shopify tomorrow. The choice depends entirely on what kind of brand the founder wants to build.

- **Choose A** if the vision is a premium, editorial brand that grows slowly and holds value.  
- **Choose B** if the vision is a social-first drop brand that blows up fast.  
- **Choose C** if the vision is a quiet, long-term luxury label with international appeal.

There is no wrong answer. These are three valid, different brands.
